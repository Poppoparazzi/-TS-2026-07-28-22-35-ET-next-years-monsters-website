# Next Year's Monsters™ — Winner-First Research Playbook v2.0
<!-- TS: 2026-10-11 12:53 ET -->

## Mission
Find what the strongest stocks had in common **before** their major 90–120-day advances, test those signals against comparable non-winners, and use validated signals to identify emerging candidates. The scoreboard is predictive usefulness, not a processed-company count.

## 1. Start with realized winners
For each rolling 90- and 120-day window, identify the top 50 qualifying U.S. operating-company stocks. Record ticker, exchange, sector, adjusted start/end prices, percentage return, liquidity, market capitalization, and exact window. Exclude data errors and non-operating securities using published, consistent rules; retain delisted stocks in the eligible historical universe to prevent survivorship bias.

## 2. Rewind without hindsight
For each winner reconstruct signals available 90, 60, and 30 days before the rally. Use publication/filing timestamps, not retrospective database revisions. Record missing data honestly. Study momentum, revenue acceleration, earnings-estimate changes, sector strength, unusual volume, catalysts, options activity, and market-cap growth runway.

## 3. Build a matched non-winner group
Select at least 50 comparable stocks matched by sector, size, liquidity and starting date that did not experience comparable gains. Identify false positives, dilution, deteriorating fundamentals and other risks.

## 4. Test original Monster DNA™ weights
- Momentum / relative strength: 20%
- Revenue acceleration: 15%
- Rising earnings estimates: 15%
- Industry / sector strength: 15%
- Unusual volume: 10%
- Upcoming catalyst: 10%
- Options activity: 10%
- Market-cap growth potential: 5%

Treat these as hypotheses, not proven predictors. Report factor coverage and missingness. Never infer unavailable historical analyst or options data.

## 5. Define outcome labels
- Historical Monster: at least +50% adjusted price gain in 90 days.
- Breakout Monster: at least +100%.
- Exceptional Monster: at least +200%.
- Emerging Monster: prospective research candidate meeting tested early-signal criteria, not an achieved return or guarantee.

Record benchmark-relative performance and drawdowns alongside headline returns.

## 6. Identify the Tipping Point™
Find the earliest point-in-time date when a reproducible combination of signals triggered. Record signal date, contemporaneous price, subsequent return, and maximum drawdown. Separate early identification from chasing an already completed move.

## 7. Validate before claiming predictive value
Compare original and alternative scoring weights on a training period, then evaluate on later untouched out-of-sample periods. Include delisted securities, realistic data availability, turnover, liquidity constraints, and transaction-cost sensitivity. Measure precision among top-ranked candidates, recall of subsequent winners, false positives, relative returns, and drawdowns.

## 8. Produce a separate Monster Discovery Score™
Develop a versioned 0–100 research score with factor contributions, source dates, missing-evidence flags and risks. Keep historical Monster Rating™ production scores and demonstration VCL™ material distinct. Never label research scores as current production ratings.

## 9. Do not block research on the 779-count pipeline
Run the historical winner/non-winner study from independently verifiable data exports and SEC filings. The Render deployment and existing rating pipeline are a separate repair track. Preserve the existing approved homepage and production records.

## 10. Milestones
1. 50 verified historical winners with adjusted prices, dates, returns, sectors and source provenance.
2. Point-in-time factor records for those winners and 50 matched non-winners.
3. Comparative report identifying which Monster DNA factors did and did not work.
4. Out-of-sample tested discovery score, with false-positive and drawdown reporting.
5. Weekly ranked emerging-candidate research watchlist, clearly marked as prospective.

## Definition of success
Identify promising companies before their large advances, explain why they qualified using information available at the time, and publish both successful and failed test outcomes. Historical results do not guarantee future returns.
