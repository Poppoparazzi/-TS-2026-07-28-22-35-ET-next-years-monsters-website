## 1. Purpose of This Document

This Product Bible is the controlling statement of what Next Year’s Monsters™ is, what it is not, what the public may be told, and what engineering must preserve.

Every contributor must read this document before changing product behavior, scoring logic, public claims, data labels, page structure, or Captain Breakout™ artwork.

When old code, mockups, issue comments, pull requests, or earlier documentation conflict with this document, the conflict must be identified and resolved deliberately. It must not be hidden inside an implementation decision.

Existing pull requests are not automatically approved merely because they were opened before this document existed. They must be reviewed against this Product Bible before merge.

## 2. Mission

> **Next Year’s Monsters™ helps investors search for today’s companies whose verified evidence resembles characteristics that appeared before previous market leaders made their greatest advances.**

The product does not claim to know the future.

It does not guarantee winners.

It searches for evidence.

### Core principle

> **The screen does not find guaranteed winners. It finds evidence.**

Every public page, score, graphic, API response, and marketing statement must remain consistent with that principle.

---

## 3. What the Product Is

Next Year’s Monsters™ is an evidence-discovery and educational research system.

It organizes verified company, filing, market, business, and risk evidence into a framework designed to help users study whether a company may be developing characteristics associated with emerging market leadership.

It connects three ideas:

1. Historical companies left recognizable evidence before their largest advances.
2. Current companies can be examined for comparable characteristics.
3. Similar evidence does not establish the same future outcome.

### What the product is not

Next Year’s Monsters™ is not:

- a guaranteed-winner service;
- personalized financial advice;
- a price-target service;
- a day-trading signal service;
- a generic quote terminal;
- a current-news publisher without a connected licensed source;
- a popularity ranking;
- a list of the largest or most famous companies;
- a casino, meme-stock promotion, or hype engine.

The site may provide quotes, charts, external stories, and research context when connected providers permit it. Those tools support the evidence system. They do not replace its mission.

## 4. The Evidence and Fingerprint Philosophy

The fingerprint is the central symbol of Next Year’s Monsters™.

Past Monster stocks were not identical. They came from different industries, sizes, technologies, and market environments. Some nevertheless displayed recurring characteristics before their major advances.

The fingerprint represents:

- recurring evidence;
- historical resemblance;
- recognizable patterns;
- investigation;
- Monster DNA™;
- the difference between evidence and certainty.

The fingerprint means:

> **The evidence visible today may resemble characteristics that appeared before earlier market leaders made major advances.**

It does not mean:

> “The company will definitely repeat the same outcome.”

### Historical comparison boundary

The product may claim a numerical “historical fingerprint match” only when a documented, tested, reproducible comparison model actually performs that calculation.

Until such a model is validated, approved language includes:

- historical resemblance;
- evidence alignment;
- shared characteristics;
- similar documented patterns.

The system must not manufacture a “closest historical match” merely because two companies look similar to a person, model, or mockup.

## 5. Captain Breakout™

Captain Breakout™ is the official guide, investigator, scout, and teacher for Next Year’s Monsters™.

He does not predict the future or promise that a company will become a Monster. He directs attention toward evidence worth examining.

### What Captain Breakout™ represents

- disciplined investigation;
- evidence before opinion;
- curiosity before excitement;
- preparation before a breakout;
- confidence without recklessness;
- the search for companies that are not Monsters yet but may be developing Monster characteristics.

### Approved identity

Captain Breakout™ must remain immediately recognizable across the website, books, applications, videos, presentations, and marketing.

His approved identity includes:

- green fuzzy appearance;
- the established face and body proportions;
- black-and-gold costume;
- dollar-sign belt buckle;
- `CB` markings on the handcuffs;
- cape where appropriate;
- confident, determined, approachable expression;
- association with evidence, upward movement, breakouts, and financial opportunity.

New poses are permitted. A new identity is not.

He must never be replaced by:

- a generic human spokesman;
- an insurance-salesman-looking character;
- a different green monster;
- an unrelated mascot wearing Captain Breakout branding;
- an excessively angry, threatening, or grotesque redesign.

His expression should communicate:

> **“Come look at this. I found evidence worth examining.”**

### Approved roles by page

**Homepage:** Captain Breakout™ rides the approved black bull and points upward. The homepage bull-rider concept remains protected.

**Monster Check™ and Monster Results™:** Captain Breakout™ becomes the investigator. He uses a magnifying glass containing a fingerprint and points toward the company evidence.

**Top Monsters™:** He becomes the scout, directing attention toward companies with the strongest verified current evidence.

**VCL™:** He becomes the teacher and historical guide.

### Signature magnifying-glass image

Captain Breakout™ holding a magnifying glass containing a fingerprint visually explains the product:

- Captain Breakout™ is investigating;
- the magnifying glass represents examination;
- the fingerprint represents evidence and Monster DNA™;
- the report displays what was found.

This should become a recurring brand image, especially on the standard Monster Results™ experience.

## 6. Monster DNA™

Monster DNA™ is the organized framework of evidence patterns that may appear before a company becomes a major market leader.

It is a branded analytical metaphor, not a biological claim.

Potential Monster DNA™ evidence may include:

- revenue acceleration;
- earnings improvement;
- expanding margins;
- improving cash flow;
- increasing demand;
- product or service differentiation;
- emerging market leadership;
- management execution;
- institutional interest;
- relative strength;
- constructive price and volume behavior;
- breakout preparation;
- a meaningful Tipping Point™;
- favorable Market Weather™;
- a measurable Move Driver™;
- the early stages of a Monster Climb™;
- risk and deterioration signals that weaken the case.

A company does not need to resemble every historical Monster or satisfy every category. The question is whether enough important evidence is appearing together to justify closer study.

Any numerical Monster DNA™ alignment must come from a documented production calculation. It must not be invented from a visual impression.

## 7. Monster Check™

Monster Check™ is the primary company-investigation tool.

It answers:

> **“What verified evidence does this company display now, and is that evidence consistent with developing Monster characteristics?”**

Monster Check™ is not restricted to the 15 VCL™ companies. It should support every eligible company in the approved coverage universe as data and processing allow.

### Search behavior

A visitor may enter a ticker and, where supported, a company name. The system must resolve the company identity before presenting company-specific SEC evidence or a Production Monster Rating™.

### Truthful outcomes

Monster Check™ may return:

- a valid Production Monster Rating™;
- **Demonstration Rating**;
- **Not Yet Rated**;
- **Unresolved SEC Identity**;
- **Provider Not Connected**.

It must never invent a placeholder score merely to make the result page look complete.

### Explanation requirement

A number alone is not a complete Monster Check™ result.

Every production result should explain:

- what evidence is present;
- what evidence is missing;
- the strongest positive drivers;
- the most important risks;
- data freshness;
- data completeness;
- source categories;
- scoring-engine version;
- calculation timestamp.

> **The score attracts attention. The evidence earns trust.**

## 8. Monster Results™

Monster Results™ is the repeatable company report displayed after a Monster Check™ search.

### Standard desktop layout

**Captain Breakout™ area, approximately 30–35%**

- approved Captain Breakout™ artwork;
- confident, approachable expression;
- magnifying glass containing the fingerprint;
- concise evidence-first message;
- no large unused black wall.

**Evidence area, approximately 65–70%**

- ticker and company identity;
- current result status;
- valid rating when available;
- Monster DNA™ evidence;
- Tipping Point™;
- Market Weather™;
- Move Driver™;
- Monster Climb™;
- Official SEC Evidence;
- external market context;
- risk evidence;
- completeness and freshness;
- chart or related tools where legitimately available.

### Responsive behavior

On tablets and phones:

- the sections stack vertically;
- Captain Breakout™ does not consume the entire screen;
- evidence remains readable;
- cards and charts do not overflow;
- buttons remain usable;
- ordinary content does not require horizontal scrolling.

Monster Results™ must be implemented as responsive application content, not one giant flattened dashboard image.

A concept image may guide composition. Its invented prices, scores, dates, news, and rating history must never be copied into production.

## 9. Top Monsters™

Top Monsters™ identifies companies displaying the strongest verified current Monster evidence within the covered universe.

It answers:

> **“Which covered companies currently show the strongest verified evidence alignment?”**

Top Monsters™ is not:

- the VCL™ list;
- a list of the largest companies;
- a list of the most popular stocks;
- a permanent favorites list;
- a set of guaranteed winners;
- a ranking built from Demonstration Ratings.

A production ranking should be driven by current Production Monster Ratings™ calculated under the same active scoring-engine version and sufficient data standards.

Each ranked company should show:

- current Production Monster Rating™;
- rating status;
- evidence completeness;
- calculation timestamp;
- strongest positive evidence;
- principal risk;
- link to the complete Monster Results™ report.

Companies using Demonstration Ratings must not be mixed into a production ranking as though all numbers were calculated by the live engine.

## 11. Monster Rating™

The Monster Rating™ is the central production score.

It is:

> **A documented, reproducible measure of the strength, quality, completeness, freshness, and timing of verified evidence associated with a company’s potential Monster development.**

Where a validated historical-comparison model exists, the rating may also incorporate documented evidence alignment with historical precedents.

### The Monster Rating™ is not

- a prediction of future return;
- a probability that the stock will rise;
- a buy or sell instruction;
- a target price;
- a guarantee that the company will become a Monster;
- a score based only on popularity, company size, low share price, or recent momentum.

A rating of 90 must never be described as a 90% probability of success.

### Intended horizon

The product is designed to search for evidence relevant to approximately the next year. That is a research horizon, not a promised deadline.

The rating asks:

> **“How strong is the verified evidence that this company may be developing characteristics of a future market leader?”**

It does not answer:

> “How much will this stock rise next year?”

## 12. Production Rating Requirements

A Production Monster Rating™ may be displayed only when the system has:

- a resolved company identity;
- sufficient Official SEC Evidence;
- sufficient required external market or fundamental evidence;
- acceptable freshness;
- acceptable completeness;
- a documented scoring-engine version;
- a reproducible calculation;
- a stored calculation timestamp;
- source provenance;
- recorded missing evidence;
- recorded risk penalties.

When production requirements are not met, **Not Yet Rated** is more truthful than a decorative number.

### Major evidence families

The production model may include:

- Monster DNA™;
- Tipping Point™;
- Market Weather™;
- Move Driver™;
- Monster Climb™;
- revenue and earnings acceleration;
- margin and cash-flow improvement;
- business quality;
- price and volume behavior;
- relative strength;
- breakout readiness;
- liquidity and tradability;
- deterioration and risk;
- data completeness and freshness.

The final weights, thresholds, formulas, exclusions, and penalties belong in a versioned methodology specification and automated tests.

### Low price is not evidence of quality

A low share price alone must not increase the rating.

Low-priced and emerging companies may offer opportunity, but they can also carry greater liquidity, dilution, reporting, execution, and manipulation risk.

### Negative evidence is mandatory

Risk evidence must be visible and capable of weakening the result.

Potential penalties include:

- falling revenue;
- deteriorating margins;
- repeated dilution;
- excessive debt;
- going-concern language;
- unreliable or stale reporting;
- unresolved legal or regulatory risk;
- poor liquidity;
- extreme volatility;
- failed breakouts;
- declining relative strength;
- customer concentration;
- promotional claims unsupported by filings.

## 13. Scoring Governance

### Determinism

The same verified inputs, scoring-engine version, and calculation rules must produce the same result.

The score may not depend on undocumented human or model impressions.

### Versioning

Every production rating must store:

- scoring-engine version;
- calculation timestamp;
- source-data timestamps;
- component values;
- penalties;
- completeness state;
- confidence state where used;
- final score.

A methodology change requires a new engine version.

### Missing data is not negative data

The system must distinguish among:

- negative evidence;
- neutral evidence;
- unavailable evidence;
- stale evidence;
- disconnected provider;
- unresolved company identity.

Missing data must not silently become a zero in every component.

When essential evidence is missing, the correct result may be **Not Yet Rated**.

### No manual score decoration

A score may not be increased because a company is exciting, popular, famous, owned by a stakeholder, or favored by a contributor.

A score may not be decreased because a company is unfamiliar.

Any future override process must be explicit, authorized, logged, explained, and visibly distinguishable. Silent overrides are prohibited.

### Recalculation

Ratings may be recalculated after:

- new SEC filings;
- updated financial results;
- meaningful company evidence;
- refreshed market data;
- provider corrections;
- a new scoring-engine version;
- a scheduled batch refresh.

The displayed rating must show when it was calculated.

### Rating history

Rating history may be shown only when actual prior production calculations were stored with their timestamps and engine versions.

The system must not manufacture history from the current rating or from a mockup.

## 14. Rating Tiers

Tier labels are presentation labels applied after a valid production score is calculated.

The currently discussed upper tiers contain an unresolved overlap:

- Gold: 85–92;
- Platinum: 92–100.

A score of 92 cannot belong unambiguously to both tiers.

Until an exact boundary is approved and documented, production code must not silently choose one. The numerical score remains controlling.

Demonstration examples may preserve approved book labels, but their **Demonstration Rating** status must remain unmistakable.

## 15. Controlled Evidence and Status Labels

The following labels are controlled public language and must be used exactly where applicable:

- **Official SEC Evidence**
- **External Market Data · May Be Delayed**
- **Demonstration Rating**
- **Not Yet Rated**
- **Unresolved SEC Identity**
- **Provider Not Connected**

Developers must not replace the actual state with softer but misleading language such as “Almost Ready,” “Live Preview,” “Estimated Rating,” or “Temporary Score.”

Truthful inconvenience is better than polished fiction.

### Official SEC Evidence

This label applies to evidence obtained from official SEC records, including filed financial statements, registration statements, current reports, ownership filings, filing dates, official identifiers, and filed risk factors.

It does not mean the SEC endorses the company, the rating, or Next Year’s Monsters™.

### External Market Data · May Be Delayed

This label applies to information obtained from external providers, including price, volume, charts, market capitalization, exchange data, relative-strength inputs, stories, and other licensed fields.

The product must not claim real-time data unless the connected provider and license actually support it.

### Derived evidence

Growth rates, component scores, risk penalties, evidence classifications, completeness measures, Monster DNA™ alignment, and Monster Ratings™ are derived outputs.

Derived evidence should retain source provenance, calculation version, timestamp, and applicable data period.

### Demonstration Rating

A Demonstration Rating is an educational or design example, not a Production Monster Rating™.

It must not be used in production rankings, current performance claims, production totals, or fabricated rating history.

The word “Demonstration” must remain visible, not buried in a tooltip or footer.

### Not Yet Rated

Use when the company is recognized but a valid production rating does not exist because processing is incomplete, evidence is insufficient, information is stale, or the current engine cannot reproduce a valid score.

### Unresolved SEC Identity

Use when the ticker or company cannot be confidently matched to the correct SEC registrant.

The system must not guess the identity to continue processing.

### Provider Not Connected

Use when a required provider is unavailable, unconfigured, or unauthenticated.

The interface must not substitute invented market fields or ratings.

## 16. Freshness, News, Quotes, and External Content

Every production result should distinguish among:

- latest SEC filing date;
- latest reported financial period;
- latest external market-data timestamp;
- latest rating calculation;
- latest successful provider connection.

A new page load does not make old data current.

### Current news

Current news may appear only when connected to a reliable, identified, appropriately licensed, timestamped source.

The product must not:

- invent headlines;
- present rumors as fact;
- create a fake catalyst;
- imply an old event happened today;
- display demonstration news as current production content.

When verified current news is unavailable, omit the section or explain the provider state. An empty decorative news panel is not useful.

### Licensing

Quotes, charts, stories, analyst-derived fields, and other third-party content may be displayed only under permitted provider terms.

Public availability does not automatically permit unrestricted commercial reuse.

Blocked external widgets must fail gracefully and provide truthful alternatives where available.

## 17. Approved and Prohibited Claims

### Approved core language

> **The screen does not find guaranteed winners. It finds evidence.**

> **Find Tomorrow’s Market Leaders Today.**

> **You can’t buy history, but you can learn its fingerprints.**

> **The evidence supports closer study. It does not establish a future outcome.**

> **Monster Rating™ measures the current strength of documented evidence. It does not predict a specific return.**

### Prohibited claims

Do not claim:

- “Guaranteed winner”;
- “This stock will rise”;
- “This company will become the next Monster”;
- “Certain breakout”;
- “Risk-free opportunity”;
- “Buy before it explodes”;
- “This score predicts next year’s return”;
- “A rating of 90 means a 90% chance of success”;
- “The SEC approves this company”;
- “Official SEC Evidence means SEC endorsement”;
- “Real-time” when the provider is delayed;
- “Live rating” when the score is a demonstration;
- “Historical rating” without stored prior production calculations;
- “Current news” without a verified current source;
- “Top Monster” when the production engine did not rank the company.

The product may describe evidence as strong, developing, incomplete, weakening, improving, historically similar, or worthy of closer study.

### Causal language

Do not state that historical evidence automatically “leads to success.”

Preferred wording:

> **These characteristics appeared before several historical market leaders made major advances. Their presence today does not guarantee the same result.**

## 19. Engineering Rules

### Inspect before modifying

Before starting material work, inspect:

- current `main`;
- open pull requests;
- recent commits;
- active deployments;
- active SEC and rating batches;
- database migrations;
- controlling issues and audit files;
- relevant tests and workflow results.

### Focused pull requests

Each pull request should have one clear purpose. Do not mix unrelated redesigns, migrations, scoring changes, copy changes, and branding changes merely because the files are nearby.

### Completion standard

A feature is not complete merely because:

- code exists on a branch;
- a pull request was opened;
- a mockup looks finished;
- localhost works;
- one test passed;
- a deployment started.

A live production claim requires applicable tests, merge to `main`, completed deployment, verification on the public custom domain, relevant API checks, and failure-state checks.

### Do not interrupt processing

Do not merge, redeploy, restart, or migrate while an active SEC batch, rating batch, deployment, or database operation could be damaged. Check current state first.

### No hidden fallback fiction

Acceptable failure states include:

- **Not Yet Rated**;
- **Provider Not Connected**;
- **Unresolved SEC Identity**;
- **External Market Data · May Be Delayed**;
- a verified cached result with its original timestamp.

Unacceptable fallbacks include invented scores, quotes, current news, rating history, timestamps, company identities, or production totals.

### Production decision order

The engine should:

1. resolve the company;
2. verify required providers;
3. collect and timestamp evidence;
4. evaluate completeness and freshness;
5. calculate components;
6. apply risk penalties;
7. calculate the final score;
8. store evidence, provenance, and engine version;
9. display the score with explanation;
10. otherwise display the correct truthful status.

The system must never begin with a desired score and work backward to justify it.

## 22. Change Control

The following are product-level decisions and require explicit owner approval before alteration:

- the meaning of Monster Rating™;
- the approximately one-year research horizon;
- the evidence-first philosophy;
- the relationship among Monster Check™, Top Monsters™, and VCL™;
- Captain Breakout’s identity;
- the homepage bull-rider concept;
- the fingerprint and magnifying-glass concept;
- controlled status labels;
- production-versus-demonstration separation;
- Gold and Platinum tier boundaries;
- core slogans;
- claims about what the product can and cannot do.

Routine bug fixes do not require product reapproval. Changes to the product’s meaning do.

### Methodology changes

A scoring-methodology change must document:

- reason for the change;
- old and new engine versions;
- affected components;
- expected rating effect;
- validation and test results;
- migration or recalculation plan;
- whether historical scores changed.

The scoring engine must not be quietly altered to make favored companies score higher.

Backtesting may inform methodology improvements. It must not be abused to make historical winners look perfect while failures disappear.

## 23. Privacy, Security, and Responsible Presentation

The product should collect only information necessary to provide the service.

Potential account data may include saved tickers, watchlists, user preferences, subscription status, usage, and technical diagnostics.

User watchlists and searches must not be publicly exposed without explicit action.

Secrets, API keys, database credentials, and provider tokens must never be committed to the public repository.

Next Year’s Monsters™ is an educational and research product. It should explain that investing involves risk, smaller companies may carry greater risk, evidence may be incomplete or delayed, ratings are not personalized financial advice, and users remain responsible for their decisions.

Disclaimers must be visible and understandable. They must not be used as an excuse for reckless claims elsewhere.

---

## 24. Accessibility

Important information must not be communicated only through color.

Status labels, buttons, forms, charts, and evidence panels should include meaningful accessible text.

The interface should support keyboard navigation, screen readers, text enlargement, and readable contrast.

Captain Breakout™ artwork should use descriptive alternative text appropriate to its page role.

---

## 25. Final Governing Principle

When the team must choose between an exciting presentation and a truthful presentation, truth wins.

When the team must choose between displaying a number and admitting evidence is incomplete, evidence integrity wins.

When the team must choose between rushing a rating and preserving reproducibility, reproducibility wins.

> **Never make the product look smarter than the evidence allows.**
