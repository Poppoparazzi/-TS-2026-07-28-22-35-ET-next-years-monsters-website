# Six-Month Monster Retrospective Calibration — Development Set

## Purpose

This is a **retrospective development/calibration set**, not a validation cohort.

We deliberately know the outcomes already. Therefore these stocks may be used to discover useful features, failure modes, and Anti-DNA signals, but they may **not** be used to claim Monster DNA V2 predicts future returns.

The prospective Version 2 cohort remains separate and must be frozen before its future returns are known.

## Historical-evidence rule

For each winner, reconstruct only what was knowable **on or before March 29, 2026**. Later earnings, later guidance, later clinical results, and later price action are outcomes, not inputs.

Each winner must be paired with at least one same-period non-winner/control chosen from a similar sector, approximate size, and business condition. The goal is to identify features that distinguish winners from plausible look-alikes rather than merely cataloging winner traits.

## Preliminary pre-period DNA observations

These are first-pass observations from primary company releases available before the six-month window. They are not yet final V2 scores.

### DELL — strong fit

Before the window, Dell reported FY26 revenue up 19%, Q4 revenue up 39%, EPS growth, record cash flow, and a $43 billion AI backlog. Management guided FY27 revenue growth of 23% at the midpoint and EPS growth materially faster than revenue.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA: strong
- Inflection & Expectations: strong, because backlog/order acceleration and raised growth outlook were visible
- Market Confirmation: must be reconstructed from March 2026 prices
- Anti-DNA: valuation and margin quality still require contemporaneous review

### MU — exceptionally strong fit

On March 18, before the test window, Micron reported Q2 revenue of $23.86B versus $13.64B in the prior quarter and $8.05B a year earlier, record profit/cash flow, and guided Q3 revenue to $33.5B with approximately 81% gross margin.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA: exceptionally strong
- Inflection & Expectations: exceptionally strong
- Clear quarterly acceleration that the old annual-heavy engine would underweight
- Market Confirmation: must be reconstructed as of March 29

### CRWD — strong fit

On March 3, CrowdStrike reported ARR growth of 24%, record net-new ARR with 47% year-over-year growth, positive GAAP net income, and record operating/free cash flow.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA: strong
- Inflection & Expectations: strong
- Improving profitability plus accelerating net-new ARR is exactly the kind of inflection V2 should reward

### AMD — strong fit, but not an early-stage company

Before the window, AMD reported 2025 revenue growth of 34%, data-center revenue growth of 32%, client/gaming growth of 51%, record Q4 revenue and earnings.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA: strong
- Inflection: strong
- Classification warning: mature/large-cap status means it may be a "major mover" rather than a classic undiscovered Future Monster. Version 2 should distinguish these use cases.

### HPE — strong inflection fit

On March 9, HPE said Q1 orders increased double digits across all segments, networking was strong, Cloud & AI profitability improved, and the company raised its fiscal 2026 outlook.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA: moderate-to-strong
- Inflection & Expectations: strong
- This is a useful example of why guidance/outlook changes deserve explicit weight.

### PANW — solid fit

On February 17, Palo Alto Networks reported revenue growth of 15%, Next-Generation Security ARR growth of 33%, and RPO growth of 23%.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA: strong quality and recurring demand
- Inflection: positive, though less explosive than MU/DELL
- Market/valuation gate becomes important because mature cyber leaders can already carry substantial expectations

### DDOG — mixed but instructive fit

On February 10, Datadog reported Q4 revenue growth of 29%, strong growth in $1M+ ARR customers, and substantial free cash flow. However, full-year 2026 guidance implied roughly 18%-20% revenue growth.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA: strong
- Inflection & Expectations: mixed because the forward guide decelerated from recent reported growth
- The fact that DDOG later became a six-month winner makes it a useful challenge case: V2 must not over-penalize ordinary conservatism in guidance.

### FTNT — solid quality / improving-demand fit

On February 5, Fortinet reported Q4 revenue growth of 15%, product revenue growth of 20%, billings growth of 18%, Unified SASE billings growth of 40%, and very high operating margins.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA: strong
- Inflection & Expectations: positive
- Demand mix and billings acceleration may be more predictive than total revenue growth alone.

### ILMN — weak classic-DNA fit; possible turnaround category

On February 5, Illumina reported only 5% Q4 revenue growth and flat full-year revenue, though margins and EPS improved and management described improving second-half momentum.

Likely V2 signals:
- Business DNA under a classic high-growth framework: weak-to-moderate
- Inflection: improving but not explosive
- This may be a turnaround/re-rating Monster rather than a classic growth Monster.
- Version 2 may need a separate "turnaround inflection" archetype instead of forcing every winner into one DNA template.

### MRNA — catalyst/event-driven category

Before the window, Moderna still had weak current commercial economics but a large late-stage pipeline, oncology readouts expected in 2026, multiple launch opportunities, and substantial cost reduction.

Likely V2 signals:
- Classic Business DNA: weak-to-mixed
- Catalyst runway: very high
- This may be an event-driven biotech Monster, not a conventional operating-growth Monster.
- A single universal score may need archetype awareness so binary clinical catalysts do not masquerade as ordinary revenue acceleration.

## Early lesson

The six-month winner set already suggests at least three distinct Monster archetypes:

1. **Operating acceleration Monsters** — MU, DELL, CRWD, AMD
2. **Recurring-demand / platform reacceleration Monsters** — PANW, DDOG, FTNT, HPE
3. **Catalyst / turnaround Monsters** — MRNA, ILMN and potentially some semiconductor/storage names depending on the March baseline

Version 2 should preserve one common discipline — business strength, expectations/inflection, market confirmation, and Anti-DNA — while allowing archetype-specific evidence inside each pillar.

## Required next step: matched non-winners

For every winner above, choose at least one same-period control that looked plausible at March 29 but failed to produce a Monster move.

Compare:
- latest-quarter acceleration
- guidance revisions
- estimate revisions
- backlog / bookings / ARR / customer adoption
- margin direction
- cash conversion
- valuation versus expected growth
- price/volume and relative strength
- sector regime
- concentration, dilution, leverage, regulatory, or execution Anti-DNA

A trait only becomes useful Monster DNA if it is meaningfully more common or stronger in winners than in matched non-winners.

## Anti-leakage rule

No data published after March 29, 2026 may be used to score the starting DNA of this calibration set. Later data may only be used to explain the realized path after the fact.
