# Next Year’s Monsters™ — Four-Tier Candidate System

TS: 2026-08-08 06:41 ET

## Purpose
The website is designed to identify companies that are not yet established Monsters but currently show combinations of evidence similar to historical VCL™ companies before their major advances. The tiers organize current candidates by strength of evidence and preserve movement over time.

## Tier 1 — Top 15 Strongest Fingerprint Matches™
Public ranked list of the 15 strongest current candidates.

Entry requirements:
- Strong overall Historical Fingerprint Match
- Multiple independent evidence categories supporting the case
- Current SEC/earnings evidence available and sufficiently complete
- No single unresolved risk severe enough to invalidate the case
- Current business/demand evidence supports continued investigation

Display requirements:
- Rank #1–#15
- Monster Rating™ / fingerprint-match score
- Previous rank and score
- Direction of change
- Closest historical VCL™ analogs
- Strongest supporting evidence
- Biggest concern / what could prove the case wrong
- Evidence date and data-completeness score

## Tier 2 — Rising Monsters™ / On the Radar
Candidates showing meaningful Monster fingerprints but not yet strong or complete enough for the Top 15.

Typical reasons for placement:
- Strong growth but insufficient earnings confirmation
- Emerging demand or product adoption that needs another quarter
- Strong fundamentals but price/relative-strength confirmation still developing
- High potential with one or more material unresolved risks
- Recent acceleration that has not persisted long enough

These are the primary candidates to move into the Top 15 when evidence strengthens.

## Tier 3 — Monster Watchlist™
Broader research bench of companies worth continued observation.

Typical reasons for placement:
- Interesting business or market opportunity but incomplete fingerprint match
- Early-stage evidence
- Mixed financial trends
- Attractive catalyst but insufficient confirmation
- Data completeness too low for a higher tier

The Watchlist is not a recommendation list. It is the active investigation pool.

## Tier 4 — Dropped Cases™
Historical record of companies that previously qualified for a published tier but later lost enough evidence to be removed or materially downgraded.

Display requirements:
- Former tier, rank and rating
- New rating
- Date of downgrade/removal
- Specific evidence that deteriorated
- What would be required to reopen the case

Important rule: never fabricate prior status. A company enters Dropped Cases™ only after it has appeared in an official dated published snapshot. Pre-launch research eliminations are logged separately as research rejections, not as Dropped Cases™.

## Movement Rules
- Full systematic review: weekly
- Event-driven review: earnings, guidance changes, material SEC filings, major contracts, product launches, management changes, competitive shifts, major demand changes, or material price/volume deterioration
- Every published change creates a new dated snapshot
- Historical snapshots are never overwritten
- A rating change must include the evidence that caused the change

## Scoring Principle
The Monster Rating™ measures strength of the current evidence match to historical pre-breakout Monster fingerprints. It is not a probability of success and must not be described as one unless statistically validated in the future.

Core public language:
“The fingerprint does not guarantee the future. It tells us where to investigate.”

## Launch Rule
The inaugural public release should contain:
- 15 Top 15 candidates
- a populated Rising Monsters™ group
- a populated Monster Watchlist™
- Dropped Cases™ may be empty at launch because no official prior dated snapshot exists yet

After launch, companies may move up, move down, enter, leave, or become Dropped Cases™ as evidence changes.
